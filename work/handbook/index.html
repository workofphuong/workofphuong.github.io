Sex Education Handbook

For secondary students in Vietnam

Final project for a 6-session online program on global citizenship in social impact.

This was my social prototype and final project for the AFS Global You Changemaker program, a 6-session online learning experience focused on global citizenship roles in social impact, followed by an implementation plan I designed.

I learned how ideas can be created through different stories and frameworks to systematize thoughts, how to communicate effectively yet empathetically with people, and how their feedback contributes to a social project. Because it was social innovation, understanding people and being able to help them as members of society is vital yet challenging. The course equipped me with such sustainable basics and various viewpoints from youngsters around the world, while fueling my interest in continuing research in the field.

When it comes to sex education...

Sex education is not a new topic in Vietnam. Educators have been trying to include it in the experimental curriculum nationwide, especially in big cities like Ho Chi Minh City. However, the content is still sketchy, focusing mainly on the anatomy and physiology of the body and not mentioning the relationships between the sexes, sexual issues and sexual relations.

Triggered by teenagers' negative reactions to their monthly period and their lack of knowledge on protecting themselves against harassment, STIs and pregnancy, I chose an educational handbook as my project, to raise awareness about the importance of sex education and to support teenagers throughout puberty like a brother or sister who has empathy as well as relevant knowledge. The project is also aligned with the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals 3 (Good Health and Well-Being) and 4 (Quality Education), to guide the approach toward better impact.

The process

Making this handbook was challenging. I was not an educator or an expert in sex education, so it was hard to systematize the information and convey it to the audience. The time constraint and my lack of experience launching a social project also stressed me out: what to do next, how to gain enough quality feedback, and how to apply those comments back into the product.

By clearly understanding my circumstances, I started planning early and got the great support I needed. Prioritizing and grouping tasks was extremely helpful. Synchronizing the handbook theme with the presentation slides saved me time, and using Google Forms to gain feedback let me communicate effectively with various audiences. I also reached out to a sex education expert, who reviewed the handbook and confirmed that all the information was accurate. That was important and extremely valuable for an educational project aimed at children.

After launch

After 2 weeks of making the handbook and 1 week of launching it for testimonials, it got certain awareness, due to the urgent need for such documents in Vietnam. A month later, I earned the advanced certificate in global citizenship in social impact, which pushed me further on my journey to contribute more to the community and society.

Joining the course and launching this project made me think mindfully about the impact that I and my community can make on society: what I can do with the resources I have, who I can reach, and how to reach different people when I need help. I also learned to plan effectively using different frameworks, set priorities for each and create a better strategic vision for the project as a whole, to convince partners to contribute to our common goals. Since then, I have become more confident joining more projects and collaborating on other ideas, while understanding better which fields I can do best in to help people.
