Travel diary · Japan, the US and Germany

Global Footprint

Things started with Taylor Swift songs and Doraemon

Ten countries, mostly through study and exchange programs.

So far, I've explored 10 countries, mostly through study and exchange programs, including two incredible experiences in Japan and Germany. I'm fluent in Vietnamese and English, and I can get by well in Japanese and some German too. This page is my personal travel diary, where I share the lessons, adventures and eye-opening moments that have shaped me along the way.

Things started with Taylor Swift songs, and Doraemon series...

Since I was a little kid, everything I knew about the world came from my English textbook and some US-UK songs on the Internet. I was so obsessed with Taylor Swift and One Direction, and my parents were playing ABBA and Westlife all the time! When I first got to go to English class in primary school, I copied the lyrics of "The Best Day" by Taylor Swift onto the back of a teen magazine and tried my best to translate it with the help of Google Translate. (It's a sweet song about having the best day with her mom and her dad, like every other ordinary day but full of love and tiny cherishing memories.) I fell in love with translating between languages and talking to people from different countries.

I was always curious how kids in other countries lived - what they ate, what they played, and how their classrooms looked. When Doraemon first became popular on TV in Vietnam, all I could think about was being in that cherry blossom scenery once, eating those delicious snacks, and wearing those neat and cute Japanese uniforms. I started to learn Japanese to understand the cartoon better and to talk to friends from Japan. I didn't know that was the decisive step that would change my future significantly...

I went abroad for the first time to Japan!

With my Japanese, I got into the Japanese-specialized class in one of the best high schools in Ho Chi Minh City, and I was honorably sent to Nagasaki, Japan, for a 10-day study trip to reform the relationship between Ho Chi Minh City and Nagasaki. I was placed in a Japanese host family for the first time!

Nagasaki was a peaceful and beautiful city, and a special chance to learn more about the history and culture of Japan: one of the three big harbors of Japan, where I could see Holland's culture woven into international trading. It is also a place that suffered the atomic bombing in World War 2, and I could empathically feel how resilient the Japanese have been to recover and become where they are today. I was overwhelmingly amazed to see in real life all the things I had only seen on TV, and this trip fueled my motivation to learn languages.

Another thing I got to know on this trip was the concept of sustainability, in terms of the SDGs (Sustainable Development Goals), for the first time. We all joined a 2-day session hosted by the University of Nagoya, discussing and presenting different ways to implement each goal. Mine was Goal 12, Responsible Production and Consumption, and we tried to promote a more sustainable and circular way of making and using things. The idea of the SDGs meant so much to me that I have taken it into my later study and career.

Back to Japan as an exchange student

One year later, I went back to Japan for a whole year as an exchange student with the MEXT - Asia Kakehashi Project, which has funded more than 1,000 students from Asian countries over 5 years, an effort of the Japanese government to foster better relationships between Asian countries. I enrolled in a Japanese high school for the whole school year, took the subway every day to class with friends, lived with Japanese host families, saw winter snow and red leaves for the first time, and ate amazing sushi and Japanese desserts!

Being in front of a culture different from mine made me reflect deeply on my origin: what I should learn and adapt to, and what I should keep as my own background. My ways of interpreting different cultures were challenged too, and I learned to approach differences more openly and patiently, because people have historical and cultural reasons for why they do certain things. As a sixteen-year-old, I would say it was definitely not an easy experience, but I'm glad I made it through. My host family told me that Japanese people value "that one chance that only happens once" (ichigo-ichie), and they were thankful that we made that "one good chance" to meet each other. I will forever thank them for hosting a child from far away and giving me all their patience, care and love.

Go international locally...

I came back to Vietnam to finish high school and enrolled in college as a Marketing student. I chose Marketing because, especially after my exchange year, I really wanted to explore the forces behind why people do certain things and how businesses apply that to daily life. I got my first internship at a Japanese company through the METI Internship Program in 2021, and I became the English-Japanese interpreter for a Japanese architect at Vietnam Design Week 2022. I always feel good when I can use my language skills to help people understand each other - that's why I keep improving my English, my Japanese and even my business study.

I also got involved with AFS International, an intercultural program, and attended training on social impact. There I launched my first social project, a Sex Education Handbook for secondary school students, and was certified for it. The handbook has its own page: Sex Education Handbook.

Journey to the United Nations

Thanks to my social project and my active involvement in international activities, I was selected as one of 5 representatives of Vietnam to go to the United Nations to celebrate International Youth Day 2023, with a full scholarship from AFS Japan.

It was the best three days of my life. Sleeping 3 hours a day and being 'full-out' in the exciting atmosphere of New York all day long, I was so emotional and happy at the same time listening to people so passionate about making the world a better place, with a realistic optimism in their 'movements' and a willingness to connect and support others with all the resources they had. I also heard about sustainability in business for the first time at this conference. I even met an employee working in sustainable investment and found out such a field exists! Those stories fueled my passion a lot, and I have carried it into my university studies in the US.

Enrolled at University of Cincinnati

Around the time of the Youth Assembly in New York, I was also packing up to spend a whole school year as a transfer student at the University of Cincinnati (UC). UC teaches Business Analytics, one of the majors I was interested in, and I felt it would be best to end my college life in another, completely different culture, getting to know more local friends and diving into their daily lives.

I never regretted a single moment, although it was tough to be apart from my family. I learned a ton of new things, about my majors and about sustainability, and became more proactive and open to talking to people and learning from them. I suffered the cold of the Midwest, cherished the red leaves and chilly fall weather, and loved the sun in the spring.

Another exchange in Germany

In 2025, after almost two years in Cincinnati, I made another big decision: to move to Mannheim, Germany, for a short-term exchange.

I love studying, and the University of Mannheim captured me at first sight with its highly ranked business programs. I took the bus to campus, walked through a historic palace to get to class, and sat in smaller lectures where professors still used chalk and a green board. German education puts strong emphasis on fundamentals, so I spent a lot of time on the research and theory behind the concepts I applied. In many ways, I learned the how at UC, and in Mannheim I grounded that knowledge by understanding the why behind it.

My favorite course was Impact Investing and Hidden Curriculum: I have had a huge passion for sustainability since I transferred to UC but struggled to find an official course teaching it, and this one answered most of my questions about the trends, the proper measure (SROI) and the framework (a logic model with 5 levels) to capture the qualitative nature of social impact.

Beyond classes, hanging out and making international friends was the most memorable part! We exchanged food from different cultures, explored Wine Festival and Oktoberfest, and headed to the Christmas market by default whenever we wanted to hang out in winter. I love German food so bad - fresh bread and sausages, flammkuchen, and neue wein (new wine) was my to-go. With the Deutschland ticket I could hop on any regional train, and over 5 months I went to almost 20 cities in Germany and 7 nearby countries, mostly just by train. As a first-timer in Europe, it was such a huge sweet dream to see how significant the world can be.

The biggest lesson was learning to trust myself and believe that I can handle things, even in the worst-case scenarios. At the beginning I was overwhelmed by uncertainties beyond my control - the visa outcome, rising flight prices, whether I would make friends, getting stuck with my luggage. Having lived through those worries, I proved to myself that whether they came true or not, I could handle them, a mindset I will carry into future major decisions. The exchange also made me reflect on whether I see myself coming back to Europe for higher education or a career, and on my own values shaped by an Asian upbringing - how I adapt to new environments while staying true to myself.

After five months I returned from Germany with more than lively memories: new thoughts, new skills, a newfound confidence, and a better understanding of myself, which is really helpful for thinking about my future career.
